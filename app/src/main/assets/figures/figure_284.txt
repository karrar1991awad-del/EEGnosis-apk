Sleep spindles are generated in the reticular nucleus of the thalamus, and through thalamocortical neurons the cortex is triggered to generate spindle bursts.21 Extended 
cortical areas in the centroparietal regions (pyramidal neurons), most likely the whole parietal cortex and the posterior part of the frontal cortex, are involved in the generation 
of sleep spindles as recorded using the MEG.22 Lesions in the thalamocortical circuit, especially thalamus and parietal lobe, can attenuate the sleep spindles.5,23,24
