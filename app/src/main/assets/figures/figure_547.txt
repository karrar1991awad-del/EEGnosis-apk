Interictal epileptiform discharges in focal epilepsy occur more frequently during sleep, especially stage 3/4 sleep (slow-wave sleep). The discharges have a greater propensity 
to spread during sleep, and thus are often seen over a wider ﬁ  eld than discharges occurring during wakefulness and REM sleep. Therefore, EEG during REM sleep may have greater
