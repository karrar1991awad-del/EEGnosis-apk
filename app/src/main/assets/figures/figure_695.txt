Focal transmantle dysplasia is a malformation of cortical development that extends through the entire cerebral mantle, from the ventricular surface to the cerebral cortex. The 
presence of balloon cells suggests that these malformations are associated with maldiﬀ erentiation of the stem cells generated in the germinal zone.59
“Frontal absence” can be seen in frontal lobe epilepsy with epileptic foci in either the mesial frontal or orbitofrontal regions.48,55,120 Generalized absences and frontal absences 
may show similar clinical and EEG features and involve the same neuronal circuits. The neuronal system primarily involved in these seizures consists of a relatively limited 
cortico-thalamocortical circuit, including the reticular thalamic nucleus, the thalamocortical relay, and the predominantly anterior and mesial frontal cerebral cortex, with the 
cortex probably acting as the primary driving site.171 The anterior cingulate gyrus is involved in self-regulation of frontothalamic circuits and may play an important role in both 
maintenance of arousal and generalized epilepsy in humans. Absence seizures may not be truly generalized but rather involve selective cortical networks as described above.172
